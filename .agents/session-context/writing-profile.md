# Shared writing profile

Use these rules as an original house profile, not as a claim of compliance with another style standard.

## Required floor

Use this floor in every agent chat and in agent-written prose. Exact quotations, code, legal text,
and established product terms can keep their source form when changing them would change meaning.

- Lead with the result or required action.
- Make the responsible actor explicit when responsibility matters.
- Prefer active voice; use passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally de-emphasized.
- Put a condition before the instruction it governs.
- Use short, direct sentences and one main idea per paragraph.
- Use a single term for each concept. Prefer the primary meaning of a word.
- Address the reader as “you” when direct instruction helps.
- Avoid idioms, slang, cultural references, buzzwords, and claims that work is “easy” or “simple.”
- Use sentence-case headings, descriptive links, and unambiguous dates.
- Keep domain terms when they are more precise than plain-language substitutes.

## Preserve the vertical voice

The floor controls clarity, not personality. A product, client deliverable, research note, and incident report may use different tones above it. Do not flatten all writing into generic documentation prose.

## Provenance

This profile adapts public principles from the [Google developer documentation style guide](https://developers.google.com/style/), especially its guidance on active voice, global audiences, scannability, and conditions before instructions.

It also selects general controlled-language ideas described by [ASD-STE100](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html): short clear sentences, consistent terminology, and one meaning per term. It does not reproduce the ASD-STE100 dictionary or rules and does not claim ASD-STE100 conformance.
