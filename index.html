Hello, I'm Frederick Casey-Housand.

I'm an AI builder based in Oakland. Most of my work has been with people trying to do something useful: climate, accessibility, public research.

Right now I'm running TinyMacro. We build one AI employee per company: it learns how the business works, lives at its own email address, and does the recurring work the owner never gets to. I put them together one team at a time, by hand.

The bet behind it, since this is where I spend my thinking: intelligence is turning into a utility. The price of a unit of machine thinking keeps falling, and right now it's subsidized well below cost. When that settles, the interesting problem won't be who has the smartest model. It will be economics: how much finished, reliable work you can get out of the cheapest intelligence that can do the job. That problem is mostly unsolved, and it isn't answered by better models. It's answered by receipts: tested setups, proven in production, that show a given configuration does a given job reliably at a known cost. The teams holding those receipts will build what everyone else has to rent. TinyMacro is my hedge on that future, and we run the whole company on it, so every week produces more proof.

I grew up in Oakland. In high school I ran the local Tree-Plenish chapter. We planted over a hundred fruit trees with a team of six, and I figured out how to fundraise, run logistics, and talk a city into letting teenagers plant on public land. The work won the David Brower Conservation Award. It's also where I picked up the line I still work by: good systems should improve the world, not just operate within it.

Since then I've built AI infrastructure for Wheel the World (an accessible travel startup) and Larta Institute (a federal research accelerator running programs for NOAA and USDA). On the side right now, I'm building AI content systems for ShoplyAI. I'm finishing an Economics degree at UCLA in December 2026, with a minor in Environmental Systems and Society.
