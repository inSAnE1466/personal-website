Hi, I'm Fred.

I build agent systems, and I run TinyMacro, a small company where I find out what they can actually do. I am an environmentalist at heart. The question I care about most is whether this kind of software can help good environmental technology get used.

Why I do this

Building a business is the most interesting strategy game I know. There is always too much to tend to, arguments with no clean answer, and never enough time, money or people. I have been playing it with AI for a few years now: first building internal tools for companies while I was still in college, then consulting, then building my own agent runtime.

Right now almost everyone is building the same thing. Agents wired into every system a company owns, sold on saved time. Some of it is useful. But I am not trying to assemble a pile of AI features, wrap each one in a website, and call the result a company.

I have loved the natural world my whole life, and I think companies shape far more of it than households do. That is where I want this software pointed. At TinyMacro the work is called Green: an engine for finding where new environmental technology fits, and what it would take to deploy it there. The thesis is on the TinyMacro site.

What I think will matter

A company's edge lives in how it already works: the playbooks, the records, the exceptions someone handles by hand. You can't out-code that. An agent has to learn it from the source before anyone should compare it to the people doing the job today.

Models and harnesses will change. I want to run Pi today and something better tomorrow without rebuilding the work. The value that lasts is in tested components, the operating knowledge around them, and evidence that a particular setup solved a particular problem.

Knowledge work is much harder to hand to an agent than code. Code has types, tests, version control and decades of tickets and specs. Most company work has none of that, so building the checks is half the job.

Research is only worth keeping if its claims stay tied to their sources. Contradictions and uncertainty should stay visible. A good system answers a narrow question with the smallest set of evidence that supports it, and tells you where that evidence came from.

A person should talk to one agent. The subagents, tools and memory underneath belong to the software. Nobody should have to manage them.

Simpler systems are easier to trust. I want to be able to open the code, understand what is left, run one piece, watch what it does, and decide what happens next.

What I have worked on

At TinyMacro I build the agent runtime, the verticals that run on it, and the tests that tell me whether they work. Before that, as a contractor, I built a marketing harness for ShoplyAI that researches, writes, checks and reports on its own SEO and outreach work. For Larta Institute I built retrieval over scientific and regulatory documents for an AI pilot serving NOAA and USDA programs. For Wheel the World I built image search and CRM automation.

I am finishing an economics degree at UCLA, with a minor in Environmental Systems and Society.
