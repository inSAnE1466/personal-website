Hi, I'm Fred.

I build agent systems, and I run a small company called TinyMacro where I test what they can do. The question I care about most is whether software can help good environmental technology find the places it would actually be used.

Why I do this

I have been building internal tools for companies for years, most of it while still in college, and for the last two years nearly all of it has involved models. The work taught me that the value sits in the unglamorous parts: knowing where a company's information lives, which exceptions people handle by hand, and how to tell whether a system did the job.

I have also loved the natural world for as long as I can remember. I think the biggest environmental levers sit inside companies, in what they build, buy and run. I want the systems I build to point there. At TinyMacro we call that work Green, and the thesis is on the TinyMacro site.

What I think will matter

AI becomes useful when it finishes work a company already values. A research brief arrives when someone needs it, or a record stays current without manual cleanup. The company should not have to care how many prompts or subagents were involved.

A company should be able to adopt a better model without rebuilding the job around it. The durable part is the harness: the state, tool access, recovery and evidence surrounding the model. When a better provider appears, switching should not mean reconstructing the work.

Knowledge work is harder to hand to an agent than code. Code comes with types, tests and version control. Most company work has none of those, so building the checks is half the job. I would rather test a few claims tied to the work than maintain a large scorecard nobody reads.

Company knowledge matters only when a system can find the right material and show why it used it. I start with navigable documents and direct reads. Vector search has to beat that simpler baseline. For larger bodies of material, I am testing whether recursive language models can inspect and divide the context instead of compressing everything into one prompt.

I expect the best vertical software to come from work inside a specific industry. The exceptions and unwritten rules are where much of the value sits. I learn them by doing the work before I generalize it.

Where I have worked

I founded TinyMacro in 2025. Before and alongside it I built AI systems as a contractor for ShoplyAI, Larta Institute, a federal research accelerator running programs for NOAA and USDA, and Wheel the World, an accessible travel company. I finish an economics degree at UCLA in December 2026, with a minor in Environmental Systems and Society.
