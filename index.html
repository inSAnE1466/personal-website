Hi, I'm Fred.

I build agent systems, and I started TinyMacro to point them at something I care about: the finite resources of the natural world, and the new technology that could help preserve them.

What I am trying to do

Land, water, energy and materials are finite, and every company draws on them. Companies decide which equipment to buy, which processes to run and what to build with, and those decisions tend to last for years. My thesis is that a lot of technology that would help already exists and goes unused because nobody did the slow part: reading the specs, finding where a technology fits, and working out what it would take to put it there.

That slow part is research and document work, and it has usually needed someone's paid time for each building and each company. I think agents can take on more of it. If knowledge work keeps getting cheaper, leaving the slow part undone gets harder to excuse, and the work gets more urgent, not less.

I have loved the natural world my whole life. Building a business is the most interesting strategy game I know, but I will never feel a call to action unless the work is for something I care about that much. TinyMacro is where I am building toward it. The environmental work there is still a proposal, not a product.

What I think will matter

A decision about a new technology should rest on evidence you can open. Research is only worth keeping if its claims stay tied to their sources, with contradictions and uncertainty left visible. That is why I care about ontologies: the technologies, sites, resources and constraints as named things with links between them, not a pile of documents.

Models and harnesses will change. I want to run Pi today and something better tomorrow without rebuilding the work. So the agent runs in a container, and a kind of work is a folder of skills, templates and permissions that the same container can load.

Knowledge work is much harder to hand to an agent than code. Code has types, tests, version control and decades of tickets and specs. Most company work has none of that, so building the checks is half the job.

A company's edge lives in how it already works: the playbooks, the records, the exceptions someone handles by hand. An agent has to learn that from the source before anyone should compare it to the people doing the job today.

Simpler systems are easier to trust. I want to be able to open the code, understand what is left, run one piece, watch what it does, and decide what happens next.
