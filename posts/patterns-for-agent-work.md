title: Patterns I want to keep in agent work
description: Small units of work, evidence that can be checked, and explicit human decisions.

The patterns I want to keep are fairly ordinary software practices. Give a piece of work a clear boundary. Keep the evidence behind its output. Check the result where someone will use it.

I do not have a general success rate to attach to these practices. They are choices I want to keep testing, and they are more useful to me than a claim that an agent is intelligent.

## Make a result inspectable

A draft, a research brief or a calculation should carry enough context for someone to check it. For a number, that means its source, population, time window and method. Repeating a calculation is necessary, but it is not enough if the calculation counted the wrong things.

A source that contradicts the answer is useful. An unanswered question is useful too. Hiding either makes review harder.

## Give work somewhere to continue

I want useful intermediate work to survive the conversation that produced it. Plain files and explicit outputs make it possible to inspect a step, correct it and continue. They also make it easier to replace the model without losing the knowledge around the job.

Smaller pieces help when their boundaries correspond to something real: research to review, a draft to edit, or a calculation to check. More agents and more stages are not goals in themselves.

## Keep a human decision explicit

Review written into a workflow is an expectation. It is not evidence that review happened. The same is true of a rule against publishing or sending a message without approval.

I want tests that exercise the actual boundary and records that show what occurred. When a check misses a failure, the next version should demonstrate that it catches that failure. A growing list of instructions is not a substitute for that demonstration.

These practices leave room for judgment. They make the work easier to question, which is what I want before I depend on it.
