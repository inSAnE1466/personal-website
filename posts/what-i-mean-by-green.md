When I say Green, I do not mean helping a company look green. The upside can never be that going green is marketable. It has to be something a company can actually do, with a reason it can check.

The shortest version is this. I want Green to be an engine built to optimize and regenerate the finite resources of the earth. It would do that by doing applied research for new technology and R&D, and finding where it can be put to use. It is a proposal. None of it is built yet.

A lot of my early notes were about compliance: carbon accounting forms, questionnaires, certification. That work is real, and it may be where a company first feels the pain. But I kept coming back to the idea that the end deliverable is not simply getting a company compliant. It is bigger than that. It is the implementation of new technology, at scale.

By new technology I mean greentech, agtech, sustainability, energy, carbon removal, and projects that try to protect or restore the natural world. I think a lot of it works, and a lot of it is waiting for someone to connect it to a place that needs it.

The question I keep asking is how that connection scales. I think it takes three things.

Diligence. Look at an opportunity from different dimensions, corroborate it, and connect it through an ontology so there is evidence that it applies, including past successes that follow the same pattern.

Connection. Within reason, how do you scale the path from a technology to an application? Which other parties have to be involved?

Specialty. What implicit knowledge is required to make it work, and where does that knowledge live today?

This is why I think it matters more now. Research, assessment and a proposed scope of work used to need paid discovery before anyone would do them. I think agents and compute now make it possible to produce serious exploratory work at that depth before anyone commits. If that holds, the reason a good technology goes unused is less and less the cost of figuring out where it fits.

I do not yet know enough to call this a company. I do not know the market, the competitors, the work done inside firms, or which problems remain poorly served. Treating enthusiasm as validation would be a mistake. What I can do is build the capacity to learn it properly, and let the evidence decide what this becomes.

I care about this because I would like the most interesting technology of my life pointed at the natural world, and at conserving, optimizing and protecting its finite resources.
