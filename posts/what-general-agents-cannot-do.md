title: What general agents cannot do
description: Where a vertical agent business can hold an edge that general agents cannot.
draft: true
---
Two schools of thought come from looking at the future.

The first asks where we can gather value, unintuitively, by handing normal human inputs to agents and scaling without human bandwidth. Personalized messages are easier to send now, because the diligence of finding intent can be offloaded to an agent. Reaching out to twenty car dealerships to compare prices becomes worth doing.

The other side is the human side: finding the tasks an agent can never scale, like meetings between people and presentations. How do you build, ironically, an agent system around these "scale of one" work items?

When building in a vertical, it is important to work out what general agents cannot do. Some of it is a performance or cost edge. More important is accreditation to do certain things, and data or supply-side assets that give the service an absolute improvement over a general agent.

Signal bots can be a first step here: gathering provisional edges from rich sources and consolidating them.

For example, say I gathered a catalog of efficient environmental technology for companies with buildings, connected it to a corpus of scoped engineering jobs and case studies, then mined building specs for the companies I want to serve and created customized documents with proofs and next steps. None of that information was my own alpha, but it became a product in and of itself.

Of course, what I am describing has no moat. It is the first bucket: a repeatable, delegable task. Positioning it depends on building industry connections with companies that could supply this information, or on building licensure or accreditation into the service, either by meeting the criteria to award it in-house or by partnering with a company or government body to make the process easier.

To get there, I would catalog these research and green technology initiatives, see where they make companies money or save them time, and study how green consultancies scope projects like these, find clients, and handle the logistics: communicating with client companies, the hurdles, the common problems.

The proposition is to build a moat the way any green consulting firm would, but mechanistically, handing the delegable work to agents at a scale current human systems cannot picture. Detailed, comprehensive audits and scopes of work could be prepared for many initiatives at once, and client feedback on what actually changed could keep improving them.

Building a well-oiled machine like this would be fascinating to me. It makes me want to work as hard as possible, because I would be helping to bring about the change in the world I want to see, as efficiently as I can. I cannot think of a better way, conceptually, than auditing with AI services to find real operational improvements.
