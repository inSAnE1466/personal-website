title: What general agents cannot do
description: A draft on where a vertical agent business can hold an edge that general agents cannot.
kind: essay
draft: true
---
Two schools of thought come from looking at the future - from which items can we unintuitively gather value from, by delegating normal human inputs to that of agents, and scale without human bandwidth? An example of this could be how personalized messages are easier to send now - as the diligence/finding intent can be offloaded to an agent. Similarly, reaching out to 20 car dealerships to compare pricing becomes monetarily feasible.

The other side of this, the HITL side - is locating tasks that can never be scaled by an agent - e.g. human to human meetings, presentations, et cetera - how to build, ironically, an agent system around these "scale of one" work items?

Alternatively, when building in verticals - it is important to ascertain what general agents cannot do - e.g. performance or cost arb, but more importantly - getting accreditation to do certain things - data or supply side asset moats to the service that offer absolute improvements over general agents.

"Signal bots" can be an initial verticalization here - gathering provisional edges from rich sources and consolidating them together.

For example, if I were to gather a catalog of efficient environmental technology initiatives for built companies, and connect them to a corpus of scoped engineering jobs and case studies, then somehow mine building specs for companies in ICP, and create customized documents with proofs and next steps - none of that information was my own alpha, but it became a product in and of itself.

Of course, I am describing something with no moat, and is of the first bucket - a repeatable, delegable task. The way to position this technology relies upon building industry connections with companies that could facilitate this information, at a discount sans mass contract, or, alternatively, building licensure or accreditation into the service, either by following criteria to award such licenses in-house, or by partnering with a company/government source to make the process easier.

As for the above mechanics - it would be pertinent to catalog these R&D/green tech initiatives, see where it makes companies money or time, and see how places like green consultancies scope projects like these, pick/source clients, and engage in logistics (communications with client companies, hurdles, common challenges, etc).

The proposition here is building a moat, just as any green consulting firm would, but doing so mechanistically, scaling delegable work to agents in a manner that cannot be conceptualized with current (human) systems. In this way, incredibly detailed, comprehensive audits/SoW can be accommodated for many initiatives at once - autonomy that, in a nuanced manner, can also be powered by a feedback loop of qualitative impact from client input.
